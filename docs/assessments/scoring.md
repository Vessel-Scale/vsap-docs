# Read Assessment Scores

Scores come from the assessment’s configured answer values, category structure, scoring method, and score ranges. The visualization displays those results; a chart style does not by itself choose the scoring method.

## Interpret a result

Check the selected assessment and category, included responses, and the configured minimum and maximum before comparing values. Averaged and summed methods answer different questions. Normalized percentages help compare position within a configured score range; see the [Library scoring reference](../library/scoring.md) for formulas and examples.

Current score views can use threshold-based bars and performance bands. Labels such as At Risk, Could Improve, and Optimal are examples; the template’s configured ranges and terminology determine the actual interpretation.

Missing scores remain missing data rather than zero. Deleted questions or categories should not contribute to the active result. After changing response inclusion or reviewing new responses, recheck the displayed totals and category context.

Finalized dashboard and peer comparisons exclude in-progress assessments. Use the underlying responses and evidence to explain what a score means before proposing actions.
